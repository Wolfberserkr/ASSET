Part I

Everyone

What the platform is, who is in it, and how to get in. Four pages that every user — agent, pit manager, supervisor, or head — should read once.

1Welcome to A.S.S.E.T.

A.S.S.E.T. — Aruba Surveillance Skill Enhancement & Training — is the department's web-based drill platform. It keeps payout math, game-protection knowledge, and floor procedures sharp through short scored drills, and gives supervisors and directors an evidenced view of team readiness.

Run drills

Ten-question scored sessions drawn from the live game and procedure pools, capped at ten minutes.

Practice freely

Unlimited, unscored practice with instant feedback, plus dedicated skill trainers.

Prove readiness

Scores, accuracy, weak areas, monthly certification, and exportable compliance records.

1.1 Who uses it

The platform serves two departments that never see each other's people or results — the department wall. Everyone signs in the same way; what appears afterwards depends entirely on the role attached to the account.

No Shift Manager account exists yet — the role is built and ready if the Pit side wants a second manager without account powers.

1.2 The department wall

Surveillance staff and Pit staff never appear in each other's dashboards, leaderboards, reports, audit records, or exports. This is enforced on the server — not merely hidden in the interface — so it holds regardless of how a page is reached.

How the wall works Department is derived from the role, not stored separately. Surveillance = agent, supervisor, director. Pit = pit_manager, shift_manager, casino_manager. Every management query and reporting function re-derives the caller's department from their signed-in identity and filters to it. Shared across the wall: the question pool, the games, and the resource library. Never shared: people, sessions, answers, audit entries, notes, and benchmarks.
Important — benchmarks never mix Because leaderboards and team benchmarks are department-scoped, a Surveillance agent is ranked only against Surveillance, and a Pit Manager only against Pit. Never compare a figure from a Surveillance report with one from a Pit report and call it a gap — they are different populations.

2Signing in

You sign in with the Employee ID and password your administrator handed you. There is no public sign-up, no email address to confirm, and no self-service reset.

  1. Open assetdrills.com in any modern browser — desktop, tablet, or phone.
  2. Type your Employee ID exactly as issued. It is not case-sensitive.
  3. Enter your password and select Sign in. You land on your Dashboard automatically.
How the login works Accounts have no real mailbox. The application derives a synthetic internal address from the Employee ID and authenticates against that. This is why there is no “email me a reset link”: the address exists only inside the system and cannot receive mail. A department head sets a new password directly instead.

2.1 Lockout and failed attempts

Important — account lockout

After 5 failed attempts within 15 minutes the Employee ID is temporarily locked and the message reads “Account locked — too many failed attempts. Try again in 15 minutes.” Wait for the window to pass, or ask a department head. Every attempt — successful or not — is recorded, and heads can see failed-attempt counts per employee in the Audit Digest (§7.2).

2.2 Changing your own password

Once signed in, open Change Password from the sidebar (under Help). The change is immediate and needs no approval. Requirements are shown live as you type: at least 8 characters, at least one number, and both fields matching.

4Using the drill portal

The left sidebar is your map: Dashboard, Drill, Practice, Resources, and My History under Main; How It Works and Change Password under Help. Your Employee ID and Sign out sit at the bottom. On a phone, open it with the ☰ icon.

4.1 The Dashboard

Your home screen summarises where you stand right now: sessions completed this month, recent average score, cooldown state, per-game accuracy, recertification progress, and your rank within your own department.

Note — the leaderboard shows IDs, not names The team leaderboard lists Employee IDs only, ranked by this month's average score, then by session count. It includes active drill-takers in your department. Deactivated colleagues drop off it immediately.

4.2 Running a drill

A drill is a 10-question, 10-minute scored session drawn from across the games and the shared procedure pool. Faster, accurate sessions score higher — see §5.

Two question types

Payout drills — a bet is shown on the felt and you type the dollar payout. Answers are accepted within a two-cent tolerance, so rounding never costs you a question.

Multiple choice — rules, game-protection, and procedure scenarios; pick the correct option. Options are shuffled every time the question appears.

Good to know

The tag beside each question shows Easy / Medium / Hard — difficulty adapts to you (§5.4).

Roulette and Craps tables offer a 2D / 3D view toggle.

Leaving early marks the session abandoned: it scores 0, does not count toward your 20, and asks you for an optional reason that management can read.

Important — the abandon reason is visible to management When you leave a drill early the app offers a one-line reason box (“radio call, restroom break, technical issue…”). It is optional, but it is the only way to explain the abandon: the reason is attached to the session and shown to supervisors in the Audit Log. A blank reason leaves them guessing.

4.3 Your results

Finishing a session opens a full breakdown, so every miss becomes a lesson.

4.4 Practice mode

Practice is unlimited and separate from your record. Choose a single game, Procedures, or Mixed — All Games. Questions shuffle and cycle endlessly; there is no pool to exhaust and no cooldown to wait out.

Important — exactly what practice records

Practice never affects your score, your cooldown, your adaptive difficulty, your certification count, or the question statistics management reads. It does leave two harmless traces — a “practice started” entry in the audit log, and a count of questions answered per game that feeds remediation credit (§7.3). Practising is visible, and that is by design: it is evidence of effort, not a penalty.

Tip — practise with purpose Use the accuracy rings on your Dashboard, or ask your supervisor for the Weak Areas report, to choose what to practise. Focused practice on an assigned game also earns credit toward a Remediation assignment: every 10 questions answered in the target game counts as one credit.

4.5 Skill trainers

Beyond the games, dedicated trainers drill specific dealer skills: the Blackjack Strategy Trainer and Hand Recognition Trainers for Caribbean Stud, Ultimate Hold'em, Let It Ride, and Three Card Poker. Like practice, they are unscored.

4.6 Resources

Reference material lives here: rules and payout PDFs, video walkthroughs, a payout calculator, and the trainers — organised by game. Resources are shared across both departments.

4.7 My History

A running log of your sessions with date, status, score, accuracy, and time. Monthly progress, best score, and average sit at the top. Select any row to reopen that session's full Results breakdown.

4.8 Pit Operations

Pit managers use exactly the same portal — same drills, scoring, cooldown, certification, and trainers. The only differences are cosmetic and jurisdictional: the sidebar is labelled Pit Operations, and the leaderboard, benchmark, and every report are scoped to Pit staff.

5Scoring & certification

A small number of rules govern every scored drill. They are stated here exactly as the application applies them — this section is the reference both agents and management should quote.

5.1 Scoring

The scoring formula score = round( correct answers × 10 × time multiplier )
Multiplier: ×1.5 when the session is finished in 5 minutes or less · ×1.25 over 5 and up to 7½ minutes · ×1.0 over 7½ minutes.
The multiplier applies to the whole session at the end, not per question. Maximum: 150 points (10 correct × 10 × 1.5). Abandoned or expired sessions score 0.
Finish timeMultiplier10/108/106/10
≤ 5:00×1.515012090
5:00 – 7:30×1.2512510075
7:30 – 10:00×1.01008060
Tip — accuracy beats speed Eight correct answers in four minutes (120) beats ten correct in eight minutes (100), but six correct in four minutes (90) loses to eight correct in eight (80 — close) and badly to nine in seven (112). Rushing pays only while you stay accurate.

5.2 The 4-hour cooldown

After each completed session you must wait 4 hours before starting another scored drill. It is global — there is no per-game bypass — and it is enforced on the server, so reloading, switching device, or clearing the browser will not shorten it. The Drill link in the sidebar greys out and shows a live countdown. Practice is never blocked.

How the cooldown is measured From the completed_at timestamp of your most recent completed session. Abandoned sessions do not start a cooldown — if you abandon, you may start again at once. The countdown the browser shows is a cached copy of the server's answer, refreshed at least every 30 seconds.

5.3 Monthly recertification

The certification rule Each drill-taker must complete 20 sessions per calendar month. Only sessions with status completed count — abandoned and expired sessions do not. The month is the calendar month; the counter resets at midnight on the 1st. Falling short flags the account and raises an in-app notification to the department's management.
Important — 20 sessions, 4 hours apart Twenty sessions at a four-hour cooldown needs planning: the theoretical ceiling is six per day, but realistically it is one or two per shift. Leaving the requirement to the last week of the month is how agents end up flagged. Management can see the required pace per agent on the Completion Tracker (§6.3).

5.4 Adaptive difficulty

Each game tracks your level independently, from 1 (Easy) to 3 (Hard).

  • Step up — three correct answers in a row at your current level in that game.
  • Step down — two wrong answers in a row at your current level.
  • Bounded — never below 1, never above 3.
  • Practice never changes it — only scored drills move your level.
Note — difficulty does not change the points A hard question is worth the same 10 points as an easy one. Difficulty controls what you are asked, not what a correct answer is worth. This is deliberate: it keeps scores comparable across agents. It also means a strong agent working at level 3 and a new agent at level 1 can post the same score — read scores alongside the difficulty breakdown on the Results screen, not alone.

5.5 How the ten questions are chosen

A session is not a random ten. The draw is designed to stay varied, to lean gently toward your weaker games, and never to serve the same near-identical question twice.

The session draw, in order of priority 1. Freshness — questions you answered in your last 3 completed sessions are excluded.
2. Spread — at most 4 of the 10 questions from any one game; shared procedure questions form their own bucket, also capped at 4. Every session therefore touches at least three different buckets.
3. Procedure variety — at most 2 procedure questions from the same category (e.g. roulette_procedure).
4. No near-duplicates — never two payout questions with the same game and payout ratio, and never two questions with identical wording.
5. Weak-area lean — a game's weight is 0.5 + 0.5 × (1 − accuracy), so a 20 %-accuracy game is about 1.5× as likely as an 80 % one. Weak areas surface more; they do not take over.
Fallback — if the fresh pool cannot fill ten, the rules relax in that order (recently-seen questions first, caps last). A full ten-question session always wins over an under-filled one.
Important — Craps is practice-only

Craps questions never appear in a scored drill. The Craps game is flagged practice-only in production, so its 74 active questions are available in Practice (including Mixed) but excluded from scored sessions. This is deliberate — the team drills Craps freely until everyone is ready for it to count. Promoting it later is a single database change and needs no application update. Note that Craps procedure questions, which are shared procedure questions rather than Craps-game questions, do appear in scored drills.

5.6 Abandoned sessions

Important Leaving a drill before finishing — closing the tab, navigating away, being timed out at 30 minutes idle, or letting the 10-minute cap expire — records the session as abandoned. It scores 0, does not count toward the 20, and is visible to management with its reason. Finish the session: a low score always beats an abandon.

6.3 Completion Tracker

Certification at a glance: who has hit 20 sessions this month and who has not, at what pace they now need to work, and the exceptions you have recorded.

The four statuses

StatusRuleHow to read it
On Track20 or more sessions completed this monthCertified for the month. Nothing more is required.
Below TargetShort of 20, and the remaining shortfall needs 1.5 sessions per day or fewerBehind but recoverable at a normal pace.

How the pace is calculated needed = 20 − completed sessions this month
per day = needed ÷ days left in the month, where days left = (last day of month − today). On the final day, days left is 0 and anyone short becomes Flagged.
The At Risk threshold is needed ÷ days left > 1.5. Remember the four-hour cooldown caps a realistic day at two or three sessions.

Recert exception notes

Use Add note on any row to record why a month fell short — approved leave, medical absence, a mid-month transfer. Notes are per agent per month, up to 1000 characters, editable and deletable, and previous months are kept in the row's history. They appear in both Completion exports, so the explanation travels with the number.

Important — a note explains, it does not excuse Writing a note does not change the status, the count, or the flag. It attaches the reason to the record so that a report reviewed months later is not read as unexplained non-compliance. Write the note in the month it applies to.

6.4 Weak Areas

Where the department struggles: accuracy by game, the most-failed questions, and per-agent weakest areas — filterable by date range, agent, and game.

What Weak Areas counts Answers from completed sessions only, in your department, within the selected date range — last 30 days, 90 days, 6 months, or all time. The default is 90 days. Practice answers are never included. Accuracy for a game is correct answers ÷ answers shown for that game, not an average of per-agent rates.
Tip — read the pair, not the number A game at 55 % accuracy over 400 answers is a training problem. The same 55 % over 9 answers is noise. Always read accuracy next to the volume beside it before you assign remediation on the strength of it.

6.6 Question Editor

Supervisors, shift managers, and heads can add and edit questions in the shared pool. There is one pool for both departments: a question Angelo writes is served to Pit Managers too.

6.8 Notifications

The sidebar bell collects the two alerts that need a manager's attention: drill-takers who missed last month's target, and drill-takers whose scores are decaying.

How the two alerts are generated Missed Target — every active drill-taker in your department who completed fewer than 20 sessions in the previous calendar month. The alert key includes the month, so it clears itself when the next month begins.

Score Decay — compares the average score of the last 14 days against the 14 days before that. It fires when the drop is 15 % or more, and only when there are at least 3 completed sessions in each window — so a quiet fortnight cannot fake a decay, and a genuinely inactive agent shows up as Missed Target instead. A dismissed decay alert returns after a week if the decline continues.
Important — dismissals are personal Dismissing a notification stores that choice in your own browser. It does not clear the alert for your colleague, and it comes back if you sign in from a different computer or clear your browser data. Treat the bell as your own worklist, not a shared queue — if an alert needs joint action, agree it in person or in Remediation (§7.3), which is stored on the server.
Note — the panel refreshes every five minutes Notification data is cached for five minutes so that moving between management pages does not re-query the database each time. A change you make now may take up to five minutes — or one reload — to be reflected in the badge.

7.1 Scorecard

Month-over-month department KPIs — average score, sessions, active drill-takers, recertification compliance, and accuracy — with delta arrows, sparklines, a monthly detail table, and a per-game accuracy matrix. Six months are shown.

KPIDefinition
Avg ScoreThe mean score of every completed session in the department that month. Unlike the Team Dashboard tile, this is session-weighted: a heavy driller moves it more than a light one.
SessionsCompleted sessions in the department that month. Abandoned sessions are excluded everywhere on this page.
ActiveDistinct drill-takers with at least one completed session that month — participation, not headcount.
Recert RateThe percentage of the roster that reached 20 completed sessions that month.
AccuracyCorrect answers ÷ answers given, across all completed sessions that month.
Per-game matrixThe same accuracy calculation split by game, one column per month. A dot means no answers were recorded for that game that month.
Important — how to read the Recert Rate honestly

The denominator is today's active roster, applied to every month in the table. If the department had 6 drill-takers in March and has 10 now, March's rate is shown as “out of 10”. This keeps the trend line comparable against the team you manage today, but it means a historic percentage in this table is not the percentage that was true at the time.

For a point-in-time figure — the number to quote in a compliance conversation about a specific month — use the Completion Tracker's Compliance Records export taken during that month, or count the Session Detail sheet.

Tip — the current month always looks bad MTD is an incomplete month compared against a complete one. On the 8th, sessions will be down roughly two-thirds and the recert rate will usually be 0 %, because almost nobody has 20 sessions yet. Judge the month on avg score and accuracy, which are rate-based and comparable, and leave volume judgements until the month closes.

7.2 Audit Digest

A period rollup of everything your department did — totals by action, the most active users, security-relevant events, and failed sign-ins — over 24 hours, 7, 30, or 90 days.

PanelWhat it contains
Totals by actionEvery recorded action in the period, counted exactly: logins, logouts, self-service password changes, practice starts, drills started / completed / abandoned, question edits, user administration, and remediation events. This is where management's own actions appear.
Most active usersThe top 15 by event count, across all roles in your department.
Notable eventsThe security-relevant subset: abandoned drills, user created / deleted / deactivated / reactivated, admin password resets, forced logouts, and remediation assigned / completed / cancelled.
Failed sign-insFailed attempts grouped by employee, with the last attempt time, matched to your department's roster.
Note — failed attempts against unknown IDs The failed-login panel can only show attempts against Employee IDs that belong to your department. An attempt against an ID that does not exist — a typo, or someone guessing — cannot be attributed to a department and is therefore not listed. It is still recorded. If you suspect probing from outside, raise it with the platform administrator, who can query the raw attempt log.
Tip — a two-minute weekly security read Set the period to 7 days and look at three things: forced logouts and admin password resets you did not perform, any employee with a double-digit failed-login count, and the ratio of drills abandoned to drills completed. Anything unexpected in those three lines deserves a question.

7.3 Remediation

Assign a drill-taker a focus — one game, or Procedures — with an optional note and due date. The assignment appears on that person's Dashboard as an Assigned Practice card with a Practice button that deep-links to the right game and a Start Drill button. Progress is tracked automatically.

How an assignment completes progress = qualifying drills + practice credits, counted from the moment the assignment was created.
Qualifying drill — a completed session that included at least one question from the target game. For a Procedures focus, any completed session qualifies.
Practice credit — one credit per 10 questions answered in focused practice on the target game (or Procedures practice for a Procedures focus).
Auto-completion — when progress ≥ target and at least one real drill has happened. This drill floor means practice alone can never certify a remediation; assessment integrity is preserved.
Target — default 3, settable 1–50. Overdue is displayed when the due date has passed and the assignment is still open; it does not close or escalate anything.
StatusMeaning
AssignedOpen and counting. Visible on the agent's Dashboard.
OverdueOpen, and past its due date. A display state — still counting.
CompletedReached the target with at least one drill, or a head marked it complete by hand.
CancelledClosed by a head without completion. Use this when circumstances change; it stays in the record.
Tip — write the note as if you will not be there The note is the only context the agent gets. “Roulette split payouts — you missed three in the last two sessions; work through the practice set before Friday” lands. “Needs improvement” does not. Assignments, completions, and cancellations are all recorded in the audit trail, so a well-written note also documents that coaching took place.

Heads manage their own department's accounts end to end — no ticket to the platform administrator required. Six actions are available, all department-walled, all recorded.

Read before deleting

Permanent delete is for mistakes — a typo in an Employee ID, an account created twice, a hire who never started. It removes the account, its difficulty tracking, and its audit-log entries. It cannot be undone, and there is no restore.

For anyone who has actually used the platform, deactivate instead. The application enforces this for you: an account with session history cannot be deleted. Training and compliance records must survive the person leaving.

Guardrails the system enforces for you

  • You can only act on users in your own department.
  • You cannot act on yourself — use Change Password for your own password.
  • You cannot act on the other head, or any other account-managing role.
  • Every action writes an audit entry naming you as the actor. Passwords are never written to it.
  • Only the platform administrator can create or alter the heads themselves.
Important — deactivation does not end a live session A deactivated user who is already signed in keeps working until their session expires or they are forced out. To remove someone immediately: deactivate first, then force logout. In that order the account is blocked before the session is dropped, so they cannot simply sign in again.

8Management playbooks

The screens are only half the system; the routine is the other half. These are the rounds worth running, in the order that makes them quick.

8.1 The daily five minutes

  1. Bell first. Open Notifications. Anything under Score Decay is a coaching conversation this week, not next month.
  2. Team Dashboard. Scan the banners and the Last Active column. Someone who has not drilled in ten days is the earliest signal you get.
  3. Act on one thing. One conversation, or one remediation assignment. A daily habit of one beats a monthly panic of ten.

8.2 The weekly review

  1. Completion Tracker. Everyone in At Risk gets told the number they need per day. The column already calculates it.
  2. Weak Areas, last 30 days. Note the two lowest games with meaningful volume. If the same game is lowest three weeks running, it is a training gap, not an agent problem.
  3. Audit Digest, 7 days (heads). The two-minute security read in §7.2.
  4. Remediation. Close what is done, chase what is overdue, cancel what circumstances overtook.

8.3 Month-end certification close-out

  1. Three days out — Completion Tracker. Anyone who still needs more than two per day will not make it; decide now whether that is a push or a documented exception.
  2. Last working day — add recert notes for every legitimate shortfall (leave, illness, transfer, injury). Write them in the month they belong to.
  3. Last working day — export Compliance Records. This is the point-in-time evidence file; store it wherever your department keeps training records.
  4. First days of the new month — the bell raises Missed Target for anyone who fell short. Work that list; the alerts clear themselves when the next month begins.
  5. Heads — open the Scorecard once the month closes and read the completed month against the previous one, then export it for the file.
Tip — one file, every month, same place The Compliance Records export is the single artefact that answers “prove this team was trained”. Taken monthly and filed consistently, it turns a stressful audit request into a folder.

8.4 Onboarding a new drill-taker

  1. Create the account (head, User Management): Employee ID matching your existing convention, full name, a password of at least 8 characters, and the correct role.
  2. Hand the credentials over in person. There is no email delivery and no reset link.
  3. Watch the first sign-in. The five-step tour runs once; it is the best two minutes of training the platform gives.
  4. Have them change the password from the sidebar on that first sign-in.
  5. Set expectations explicitly: 20 sessions a month, 4 hours between scored drills, finish every session you start.
  6. Point them at Practice before their first scored drill, and at Resources for the rules PDFs.
  7. Note the mid-month join. A person hired on the 20th cannot reach 20 sessions that month. Add a recert note on the Completion Tracker the day they start, so the shortfall is explained before it is questioned.

8.5 Offboarding

  1. Deactivate the account in User Management — the reversible block that keeps all history.
  2. Force logout immediately afterwards if they may still be signed in somewhere.
  3. Do not delete. The application will refuse for anyone with session history, and that is the correct outcome: their training record is departmental evidence.
  4. Expect them on the roster. Deactivated accounts remain on the Team Dashboard and Completion Tracker showing 0 sessions. Add a recert note — “left the department on 14 July” — so the row explains itself for the rest of the month.
  5. Cancel any open remediation so it does not sit overdue forever.

8.6 Coaching a weak game

  1. Confirm it is real — Weak Areas over 90 days, and check the answer volume, not just the percentage.
  2. Check the questions, not only the people — Question Stats filtered to that game. A cluster of “Too Hard” flags may mean a badly worded question rather than a weak team.
  3. Assign remediation with a target of 3–5 and a due date about two weeks out. The form already suggests each person's weakest game.
  4. Tell them what you assigned and why. The card appears on their Dashboard, but a sentence in person is what makes it land.
  5. Re-read Weak Areas over 30 days after the due date. If the game has not moved, the problem is the material — take it to a floor briefing rather than assigning again.

8.7 Keeping the question pool healthy

  1. Monthly — Question Stats, sort by Shown ascending. Questions never shown are either brand new or in a practice-only game.
  2. Review the flags. “Too Hard” below 40 % after 10 showings: read the question and verify the stored answer before blaming the team.
  3. Watch the 30-question line per game. Ultimate Texas Hold'em sits at exactly 30 today — retiring two questions puts it under the minimum.
  4. Retire by deactivating, never by rewriting a question into something different. Rewriting silently changes the meaning of every historical statistic attached to it.
  5. Add in small batches and answer each new question once in Practice to prove it works.

12Security, privacy & data

A training platform that holds named performance data has to be defensible. This section states what is protected, what is recorded, and what management is responsible for.

12.1 Controls in place

ControlHow it works
AuthenticationManaged identity provider. Passwords are stored hashed by the auth service — no one, including the platform administrator, can read an existing password. Setting a new one is the only option.
Failed-login lockout5 failed attempts in 15 minutes locks the Employee ID temporarily. Every attempt is recorded.
Session timeoutAutomatic sign-out after 30 minutes of inactivity.
Forced logoutA head can end a live session; the signed-in browser checks for this roughly every 45 seconds and on window focus.
Row-level securityAccess rules live in the database, not the interface. A user's role and department are re-derived from their signed-in identity on every query, so a hand-typed address or a modified page cannot reach another department's data.
Privileged actionsCreating accounts, resetting passwords, and deleting accounts require a privileged key that never reaches the browser. They run server-side, re-verifying that the caller is an active head acting inside their own department.
Audit logAppend-only. Drill-takers have no access at all. It records who did what and when — never passwords.
Login attemptsHeld in a table with no direct read access; readable only through the department-scoped summary in the Audit Digest.
Session integrityOptions are shuffled per display, payout answers are validated against a computed value, the cooldown is enforced on the server, and every session records IP address and device.
Client storageNo performance data or credentials are kept in the browser. Only convenience state — dismissed alerts and a short-lived cooldown cache.

12.2 What is stored about a person

  • Identity — Employee ID, name, role, active flag, last session time.
  • Sessions — start and finish times, duration, status, score, IP address, device string, and an abandon reason where given.
  • Answers — every question answered, what was submitted, whether it was correct, the bet shown on payout drills, and the game.
  • Practice — a per-game count of questions answered and correct, appended when a practice run ends.
  • Audit entries — logins, logouts, password changes, drill and practice events, question edits, account administration, remediation.
  • Notes — recert exception notes and remediation notes written by management.
  • Login attempts — Employee ID, outcome, and time.

12.3 Retention — a decision management owns

Important — nothing is deleted automatically

The platform has no automatic retention or purge. Sessions, answers, audit entries, and login attempts accumulate indefinitely, which is what you want for training evidence and what you must be deliberate about for privacy. There is no “delete my data” button, and the only removal path is the permanent delete of an account that has never run a session — which also removes that account's audit entries.

If the property's records policy requires a retention limit, that is a decision to take with the platform administrator and apply deliberately. Document whatever you decide; an undocumented “keep everything forever” is a position, and it should be a chosen one.

12.4 Management's responsibilities

  • Credentials are handed over in person. Never send a password by chat, note, or shared document. Set it, hand it over, and have the person change it at first sign-in.
  • Exports leave the perimeter. Treat every downloaded workbook as a confidential HR-adjacent document (§9).
  • Use the audit trail before the accusation. IP and device data exists to confirm or clear a specific concern, not for routine monitoring of staff.
  • Offboard the same day. Deactivate, then force logout — see §8.5.
  • Review the notable events weekly. An admin password reset or forced logout that no head performed is the signal that matters most.
  • Keep the head accounts small. Account powers belong to the two heads; the platform administrator creates and changes those two accounts and nobody else should hold that access.

13Frequently asked questions

13.1 For drill-takers

Q. I forgot my password.
There is no self-service reset — the login address is synthetic and cannot receive mail. Ask your supervisor or department head; a head can set a new one in seconds and hand it to you.
Q. My account is locked.
Five failed sign-ins within 15 minutes triggers a temporary lock. Wait for the window to clear, or ask a head.
Q. The Drill link is greyed out.
You are inside the 4-hour cooldown after a completed session; the countdown is on the link itself. Practice is always available and never blocked.
Q. I was signed out unexpectedly.
Either 30 minutes of inactivity, or a head ended the session. Sign back in — a forced logout does not block your account.
Q. Does practice affect my score or my ranking?
No. Practice never changes your score, cooldown, difficulty, certification count, or the question statistics. It is logged as activity and it earns credit toward an assigned remediation, but it can never harm your record.
Q. My session says “abandoned”.
It was left before the tenth question — closed, navigated away from, timed out, or past the 10-minute cap. It scores 0 and does not count toward your 20. Finish every session you start.
Q. I answered the payout right but it was marked wrong.
Payouts are checked against bet × ratio within two cents. Check that you read the full chip stack — mixed stacks of $1 and $5 chips are deliberate. If the stored answer really is wrong, tell your supervisor: they can correct the question, and Question Stats will show it failing for everybody.
Q. Why do I keep getting the same game?
You should not get more than 4 questions from one game in a session, and questions from your last three sessions are skipped. Weaker games do surface more often — that is the point — but only about 1.5× more, not four times.
Q. Why don't I see Craps in scored drills?
Craps is practice-only for now: drill it as much as you like in Practice, but its questions are held out of scored sessions until the department decides they count. Craps procedure questions do appear in scored drills.
Q. Why don't I see the other department?
By design. Surveillance and Pit are walled off from each other's people and results. Questions, games, and resources are shared; performance is not.
Q. Can I use my phone?
Yes — the app is fully responsive. On payout drills the keyboard opens only when you tap the amount field, so the table layout stays visible while you work out the payout.

13.2 For management

Q. The Team Dashboard says the team average is 79.4 and the Scorecard says 82. Which is right?
Both. The Team Dashboard tile averages each person's monthly average, so everyone counts equally. The Scorecard averages every session, so heavy drillers weigh more. Quote the Scorecard for department performance and the Team Dashboard for “how is each person doing”.
Q. Everyone's average went blank on the 1st. Did we lose data?
No. The Team Dashboard and Completion Tracker are current-calendar-month views and they empty at month rollover. History is intact on Agent Detail, in My History, and in the Scorecard.
Q. When exactly does the month roll over?
The certification counters are calculated on the server, which runs on UTC — 20:00 Aruba time on the last day of the month. A session finished at 21:00 on 31 July counts toward August. Tell anyone chasing their twentieth session to be finished before 8 pm on the last day. See §14.1.
Q. Why is a person who left still on my roster?
Deactivated accounts stay on the Team Dashboard and Completion Tracker — with Status Inactive and 0 sessions — because their training history is retained. They drop off the leaderboard and out of notifications immediately. Add a recert note explaining the departure; see §8.5.
Q. Can I delete someone who left the department?
Not if they have ever completed a session — the platform refuses, deliberately. Deactivate instead. Permanent delete exists only for accounts created in error.
Q. An agent insists they completed a session but the count did not move.
Check three things on Agent Detail: the session status (abandoned scores 0 and does not count), the completion time against the 20:00 month boundary, and whether they are looking at a cached page — the management notification data refreshes every five minutes.
Q. Can I change the 20-session requirement, the 4-hour cooldown, or the scoring multipliers?
The 20 sessions and 4 hours are stored as configurable values and can be changed by the platform administrator — there is no screen for it. The scoring multipliers are fixed in the application; an admin settings panel is not built. Changing either affects everyone in both departments immediately, so agree it before it is changed.
Q. Why does the Question Stats accuracy differ between me and the other department's head?
Because it is department-scoped by design: the pool is shared, the experience of it is not. Your figures come only from your department's answers.
Q. Why does my Audit Log show no question edits, when I know questions were edited?
The Audit Log page lists drill-takers only. Edits made by a supervisor or head are recorded against that manager, so they appear in the Audit Digest totals instead. See the correction in §6.7.
Q. Someone's password was reset and I did not do it. How do I find out who?
Audit Digest → Notable events, over a period covering the date. Admin password resets, forced logouts, and account creations are all attributed to the head who performed them. Only the two heads and the platform administrator can perform them at all.
Q. How far back does the Audit Log go?
Everything is kept — but the page displays the most recent 500 matching entries. Narrow the date range to read older detail, and use the Audit Digest whenever you need counts rather than rows.
Q. Can agents see each other's scores?
Only as a leaderboard of Employee IDs with a monthly average, inside their own department. No agent can open another agent's history, answers, or detail page.
Q. Can a supervisor assign remediation?
No — Remediation is a head tool. Supervisors identify the need from Weak Areas and Agent Detail and ask the head to assign it, or coach directly.
Q. Can I tell whether people actually practise?
Yes. The Audit Digest counts practice starts for the period, the Audit Log has a Practice filter showing what each drill-taker practised, and any open remediation shows the drills-plus-practice split per person.
Q. A decay alert fired for someone whose scores look fine to me.
The rule is a 15 % or greater drop between the last 14 days and the 14 before, with at least 3 completed sessions in each window. It is often a real signal — a run of fast, sloppy sessions, or a shift change. Open Agent Detail and read the last ten sessions before deciding.
Q. I dismissed an alert and it came back / my colleague still sees it.
Dismissals are stored in your own browser, not on the server. They return on another device and after clearing browser data, and decay alerts resurface weekly while the decline continues. Coordinate through Remediation, which is stored centrally.
Q. Recert Rate on the Scorecard reads 0 % but people are drilling.
Mid-month it will: almost nobody has 20 completed sessions by the 10th. The current month is marked MTD for exactly this reason. Judge an open month on avg score and accuracy.
Q. Can I get a report for one specific past month?
The Scorecard gives the monthly KPI row for each of the last six months. For named, per-person detail of a past month, use the Compliance Records export taken during that month — which is why §8.3 makes it a monthly habit.
Q. An agent cannot sign in. What do I check, in order?
1. Is the account active? 2. Are they locked out — five failures in 15 minutes? 3. Is the Employee ID typed exactly as issued (case does not matter, stray spaces do)? 4. If all three are clear, reset the password from User Management and hand it over.
Q. How do I remove someone's access immediately?
Deactivate, then force logout — in that order. Deactivation alone blocks the next sign-in but leaves an open session running.
Q. Are there email notifications?
No. Alerts are in-app only, on the bell in the management sidebar. Email delivery is not built. Anyone who needs to know must be told; the platform will not tell them.
Q. Can agents game the system?
The obvious routes are closed: options are shuffled on every display, payout answers are computed rather than matched to text, the cooldown is enforced on the server, questions from recent sessions are excluded, abandoned sessions are logged with a reason, and every session records IP and device. The remaining risk is one person drilling on another's account — which is exactly what the IP and device column exists to check.
Q. What do I hand over when someone asks for proof of training?
The Compliance Records export from the Completion Tracker: sheet one is who was compliant, sheet two is every session with date, time, score, and duration. Add the recert notes — they are in the export — and the file explains itself.
Q. Something in the application disagrees with this manual.
The application is right — it is the system of record. Tell the platform administrator so the manual can be brought back into line.
